Solution Architecture Inputs and Recommendations:

1. Security Considerations

   Data Exposure Analysis (Priority):
   - The primary security concern is whether delivery data is acceptable for public exposure
   - Business and Legal must determine if the data can be scraped for price and volume analysis by competitors or third parties
   - This business/legal analysis must be completed BEFORE proceeding to technical security implementation

   InfoSec Standards (Post-Approval):
   - Data encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+)
   - Data encryption at rest (AES-256)
   - UI endpoint encryption (HTTPS only)
   - DDoS protection (AWS Shield or equivalent)
   - Injection protection (SQL injection, XSS prevention)
   - Compliance with company InfoSec standards and policies
   - Disaster recovery plan and RTO/RPO requirements
   - High availability architecture (multi-AZ deployment)

2. Business Concerns

   Strategic Implications:
   - Outsourcing core transportation functionality to a 3rd party creates vendor dependency
   - Consider future expansion of capabilities - will the vendor support additional features?
   - Evaluate synergies with other applications that may require external customer access
   - Risk of losing strategic value from direct external customer end-to-end engagement

   Standardization Requirements:
   - This solution is strictly for Japan operations
   - What happens if APAC standardization is required in future?
   - What if Global mandates linking all customer-facing portals to a single company website?
   - Document these as known limitations and future risks

3. Cost Considerations

   Beyond ROI:
   - Initial and running costs have been measured against ROI (already completed by business)
   - IMPORTANT: W3 Sirius is a legacy system with limited remaining lifespan
   - This solution should be positioned as a QUICK WIN with an impermanent nature
   - Avoid over-investment given the legacy platform it extends
   - Consider exit strategy when W3 Sirius is eventually replaced
